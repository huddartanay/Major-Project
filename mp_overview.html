Major Project · BMS College of Engineering · Oct 2026

Runtime safety supervision for a driver that can't be trusted on its own.

A learned self-driving controller gives a confident command even when its inputs are wrong. ASTRA is a nine-layer supervisor built around such a controller: it watches the sensors, estimates the car's state, checks every proposed command against an independent model and fixed rules, and — when something is wrong — steps the car down through reduced-speed modes to a stop instead of failing abruptly.

Central result — calibration is not validity. A safety monitor can meet its statistical guarantee and still be almost blind to the behaviour it is meant to watch. We measured exactly that in our own gate, and built a method to find such defects.

01

The problem a monitor is supposed to solve

A monitor that passes every calibration check looks trustworthy on paper. The question nobody in the ~95 papers we reviewed asks directly: does the calibrated monitor actually depend on the quantity it claims to monitor? Ours did not — and it still passed.

WHY A LEARNED DRIVER NEEDS A SUPERVISOR

Confident when wrong

The PPO controller emits a command every tick regardless of whether its inputs are healthy. Nothing in the learned policy knows when a sensor has failed.

THE STANDARD DEFENCE

A calibrated statistical gate

A conformal gate blocks commands that stray too far from an independent prediction, with a tuned threshold. It passed every calibration check we gave it.

WHAT WE FOUND

Calibrated, yet blind

Our gate's score is ≈87% throttle, 13% brake, 0.04% steering — but the twin it compares against models only steering. It was almost blind to what it claims to monitor, and nothing flagged that.

07

Dataset, methodology, and what we claim

There is no external dataset. ASTRA runs in our own simulator with generated sensor streams; every number here comes from recorded runs of the pipeline, not from measured hardware.

SIMULATION & DATA PROVENANCE

Own simulator, generated streams

120 s per run at 20 Hz. Faults are injected at the sensor boundary with known ground truth. Pre-registered studies use dev and held-out seed blocks (e.g. 20260731 dev; 20270101 / 20270201 held-out), each used once. No real-vehicle data, no third-party dataset.

METHODOLOGY

Pre-registered, held-out, reported in full

From STEP 6 on, hypotheses and decision rules were committed before runs. Refuted hypotheses are written up the same way as confirmed ones. Single exploratory runs are labelled as such and never quoted as rates.

What we claim

  • An audit of whether a calibrated monitor actually depends on the quantity it monitors.
  • A measured defect in our own monitor (F4) that every standard calibration check passed anyway.
  • Announced / flagged faults are handled: detect, slow, stop (F1).
  • One bad position source out of three is tolerated and excluded (measured).

What we never claim

  • "First" of anything, or resilience to all attacks.
  • Every sensor fault is detected — speed and lateral-accel defects are not.
  • Runtime learning or safe exploration is operational — both are shadow / off.
  • Real-vehicle validation, certification, or robustness to a fully-controlled input.

Literature position. ~95 papers reviewed across conformal safety gates, runtime monitors, and sensor attacks. Each individual ingredient — layered resilient architectures, tolerating a minority of bad sensors, staged degradation, online recalibration — is already published; we claim none as new. What we did not find in the literature is a check of whether a calibrated monitor is actually informative about what it monitors.

What is not done, and the plan

Known gaps

Twin & gate mis-specified

The statistical gate contributes little today (F4).

No frozen-value check

Drives the F3 blind spot.

Speed & lateral accel single-source

No cross-check is possible on those channels.

Exploration & calibration switching off

"Explores instead of stopping" is untested; recorded runs never exit exploration.

One simulator, one controller

Generality unknown.
